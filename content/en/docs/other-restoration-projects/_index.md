Other projects offer useful comparisons for Skrei's engine department restoration. Railway projects provide the clearest published accounts of engine rebuilding, while the marine projects below involve vessels related to the Nasty/Tjeld design.

Sources reviewed on **1 November 2026**. A restored vessel does not necessarily retain working Deltic engines, and older restoration plans may no longer reflect current work. Railway engine variants also differ from marine installations; their rebuild accounts are references, not procedures for Skrei's machinery.

## Deltic Preservation Society — Barrow Hill, United Kingdom

The society maintains preserved Class 55 locomotives and Napier Deltic power units. Its restoration of **power unit 430 for 55009 Alycidon** is a particularly useful engine-level case study. The unit had last run in 1981; published work includes replacement hoses and injector-pocket seals, phasing-case overhaul and generator refurbishment. Its installation and return to use were reported in late 2025.

- [Engine inspection and power unit 430 restoration report](https://www.thedps.co.uk/post/good-news-for-suspect-napier-power-unit) — a first-hand account with photographs and named specialist contractors.
- [Completed power unit installation](https://www.railforum.uk/2025/12/volunteer-led-overhaul-sees-13-ton-napier-power-unit-fitted-to-iconic-deltic/) — Rail Forum's December 2025 report.
- [Society workshop news](https://www.thedps.co.uk/news) — continuing maintenance and restoration reports, including further phasing-case work in September 2026.

The inspection report names Power Engineering Projects Ltd, Compact Orbital Gears and Dorlec as contractors involved in inspection or component overhaul. Their current services and suitability for Skrei's marine engines would need to be established directly.

## Baby Deltic Project — Barrow Hill, United Kingdom

This project owns the sole surviving **Napier Deltic T9-29 nine-cylinder engine and generator set** and is building a recreated Class 23 locomotive around it. The group acquired the power unit in 2001, began refurbishment in 2007 and successfully started the engine in October 2008. Later reports document locomotive construction, installation and generator work.

- [Project background and engine refurbishment history](https://www.babydeltic.co.uk/about).
- [April 2025 progress report](https://www.babydeltic.co.uk/news-25-april-2025) — photographs of the installed engine, with the main generator still awaiting return from overhaul at that date.
- [Project website](https://www.babydeltic.co.uk/) — further updates and project information.

## PTF-3 “Fast and Nasty” — DeLand, Florida, United States

PTF-3 is a Norwegian-built Nasty/Tjeld-type patrol boat preserved at the DeLand Naval Air Station Museum. Its restoration listing describes extensive hull rebuilding and an intention to reinstall its two Deltic engines when funding permits.

**Status qualification:** that engine statement was published in 2014. The museum continues to list the boat as an exhibit, but the sources reviewed do not establish a current engine rebuild or a return to Deltic-powered operation.

- [Restoration description in WoodenBoat's register](https://www.woodenboat.com/register-wooden-boats/ptf-3-fast-and-nasty) — dated 6 March 2014.
- [DeLand Naval Air Station Museum](https://delandnavalairmuseum.org/) — the current museum website and visitor information.

## PTF-19 — United States: historical restoration lead

A former PTF engineer's illustrated account includes a **3,100 hp Napier Deltic on a shipping stand awaiting installation in PTF-19**, together with discussion of the boat awaiting restoration.

**Status qualification:** this is a historical lead. Recent restoration progress and the present condition of the engines were not verified. It should not be listed as a confirmed active engine restoration.

- [PTF history, Deltic photographs and restoration discussion](https://www.n6cc.com/trumpy_ptf17/).

## Technical Museum — Zagreb, Croatia

The museum's 2006 report records a presentation about the research, restoration and presentation of a **Napier Deltic marine engine**, alongside an aircraft conservation project. This provides evidence of a museum restoration project, although it does not establish that the engine was restored to running condition.

- [Museum report for 2006 (PDF, Croatian)](https://mdc.hr/UserFiles/File/Izvjesca/2006/tehnicki-tekst06.pdf) — search the document for “Deltic” to find the English presentation title.

## Related vessel preservation: PTF-26 Liberty

Older descriptions of PTF-26 can make it appear to be a continuing Deltic restoration project. However, its original engines were replaced with **Detroit Diesel 16V71 engines in 2013**, according to an owner-supplied vessel history. It is useful as a vessel-preservation comparison, rather than evidence of a current Deltic engine restoration.

- [Liberty Maritime's account of its former PTF-26 project](https://www.libertymaritime.com/boats).
- [Owner-supplied vessel history documenting the engine replacement](https://ky.reel-scout.com/crewadmin_file.aspx?id=188168).
