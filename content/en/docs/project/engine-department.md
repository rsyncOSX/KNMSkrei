## Purpose and working method

Survey the complete machinery installation, establish what is serviceable and plan the work needed for safe, reliable operation. Work system by system, following each circuit from its source through pumps, filters, valves and consumers to its return or discharge. Include equipment outside the engine room where it supports propulsion, electrical power or emergency response.

Confirm the installed arrangement before specifying repairs. For each system, produce a diagram, equipment list, condition report, work package and agreed inspection and test criteria. Use the [baseline and verification plan](../planning-and-verification/) to control the work.

## Main propulsion engines

- Identify both installed engines and their accessories, configuration, serial numbers and modifications.
- Establish preservation history, last operation, operating hours and hours since major overhaul; record damage, failures and previous repairs.
- Assess accessible internal and external condition, corrosion, leaks, mountings, foundations, couplings and vibration isolation.
- Inspect fuel injection, lubrication, cooling, air intake, scavenging or charging equipment, exhaust and starting arrangements against the correct engine manuals.
- Have Deltic specialists define the inspection, overhaul and recommissioning requirements before attempting to turn or start a long-idle engine.

## Auxiliary engines and electrical installation

- Survey generator engines, generators, any shore supply, batteries, chargers and emergency power arrangements.
- Record voltage, frequency, ratings, distribution diagrams and the equipment supplied by each source.
- Inspect switchboards, cables, terminations, protective devices, earthing or bonding arrangements, lighting and emergency lighting.
- Plan competent electrical inspection and appropriate tests of insulation, protection, changeover arrangements and generator operation under load.
- Identify essential services and document what remains available following a loss of normal electrical power.

## Propellers, shafting, gearboxes and thrust bearings

- Inspect propellers for damage, corrosion, previous repairs and secure attachment; record dimensions and identifying marks where available.
- Survey shafting, couplings, stern tubes, bearings, shaft seals and stuffing boxes. Record wear, clearances, lubrication and leakage against agreed limits.
- Inspect reduction and reversing gearboxes, clutches, controls and lubrication systems.
- Identify thrust bearings and assess their condition, mounting and lubrication; establish how propeller thrust is transferred into the structure.
- Plan alignment checks with the vessel’s support and afloat condition taken into account. Coordinate underwater work and access with the docking plan.

## Fuel tanks and fuel system

- Map storage, settling and service tanks where fitted, including filling, transfer, supply, return, vent, sounding and drain arrangements.
- Assess remaining fuel, water, sludge, contamination and corrosion; plan controlled draining, waste disposal and cleaning where needed.
- Open tanks for inspection only under an approved isolation and enclosed-space procedure, including atmosphere testing and rescue arrangements where entry is required.
- Inspect tank structure, supports, access covers, piping, filters, separators, pumps and flexible connections.
- Check shut-off and emergency fuel-isolation arrangements and verify cleanliness and tightness before returning the system to service.

## Lubrication systems

- Survey engine and gearbox oil circuits, sumps, tanks, pumps, coolers, filters, strainers and pressure-regulating devices.
- Establish oil type, age, contamination and preservation treatment; use sampling where it can inform the condition assessment.
- Define cleaning, flushing, oil replacement and pre-lubrication requirements from the applicable manuals and inspection findings.

## Cooling water and seawater systems

- Map freshwater and seawater circuits, pumps, sea chests, strainers, heat exchangers, coolers, expansion tanks and drains.
- Establish how long coolant has remained in each engine and what antifreeze and corrosion inhibitors were used. Record unknowns and assess coolant condition and compatibility before reuse or replacement.
- Inspect for blockage, corrosion, leakage, freeze damage and deteriorated seals, hoses or impellers.
- Agree cleaning, leak-testing and circulation checks, including temperature and pressure monitoring during commissioning.

## Pipes, valves, sea inlets and overboard discharges

- Create a register of pipes, valves, hoses and connections, identifying their system, material, size, location and function.
- Inspect corrosion, supports, chafing, flexibility, accessibility and identification; investigate concealed or inaccessible sections.
- Give sea inlet and overboard discharge valves particular attention: inspect their bodies, hull connections, operating mechanisms and emergency accessibility.
- Coordinate opening or removal of hull-connected fittings with docking or an engineered isolation arrangement. Verify operation and watertightness before acceptance.
- Record valve positions and provide clear system diagrams and operating instructions.

## Starting and compressed air

Both the main propulsion engines and the auxiliary engines use compressed-air starting, as confirmed by the project contributor on 3 November 2026. The detailed arrangement, component condition and operating limits still require verification aboard.

- Follow the [compressed-air system survey and verification checklist]({{< relref "/docs/project/compressed-air/_index.md" >}}) for every compressor, receiver, distribution branch, starting assembly and other air consumer.
- Trace the supply to each main and auxiliary engine, including shared receivers, isolation, non-return protection and local or remote starting controls.
- Establish how starting air is replenished from a depleted system, including how the first auxiliary engine can be started and which compressors depend on running engines or electrical power.
- Record component identification, ratings, inspection history and certificates. Have a competent specialist determine inspection, testing and certification requirements before pressurisation; use the applicable manuals to define acceptance criteria.

## Machinery controls, instruments and alarms

- Inventory local and remote controls, engine-order arrangements, gauges, sensors, alarms, shutdowns and emergency stops.
- Check calibration, wiring, signal lines, interlocks and the response to loss of power or a failed sensor.
- Assess how modern sensors and monitoring can be incorporated behind existing instrument faces while retaining the historic appearance.
- Document modifications, access for maintenance and the distinction between original displays and modern protective functions. Agree essential protections before commissioning.

## Ventilation, air intake and exhaust

- Inspect fans, ducts, air intakes, dampers, closures and remote stopping arrangements.
- Assess combustion-air supply and machinery-space heat removal for the intended operating conditions.
- Inspect exhaust piping, supports, expansion joints, insulation and any cooling or water-injection arrangements; address leakage, hot surfaces and water-ingress risks.

## Fire detection and firefighting

- Survey detection, alarms, portable extinguishers and any fixed extinguishing system, including release controls and service records.
- Inspect fire pumps, any emergency fire pump, seawater supply, fire main, hydrants, hoses, nozzles and couplings.
- Verify the relationship between fire response, ventilation shutdown, fuel isolation and escape routes.
- Arrange specialist servicing and controlled functional tests. Record limitations, operating instructions and crew training requirements.

## Bilge pumping and damage control

- Map bilge wells, suctions, strainers, pumps, manifolds, non-return valves, discharges and high-level alarms.
- Verify which spaces each pump can drain, available power sources and the arrangements following a machinery or electrical failure.
- Inspect portable pumps, hoses, adapters, emergency lighting and communications used for flooding response.
- Prepare a damage-control plan showing compartments, isolation points, pumping routes and equipment locations.
- Inventory leak-stopping materials, plugs, patches and shoring equipment. Develop shoring arrangements with competent advice, including suitable structural reaction points.

## Other machinery and services to confirm

Include steering gear and its hydraulic or mechanical supply, freshwater and sanitary systems, heating, workshop equipment, lifting appliances and any other auxiliary machinery within the department’s responsibility. Record interfaces with hull, deck and electrical work so that responsibility for each item is clear.

## Safety and maintenance references

The applicable requirements depend on the vessel’s status, certification and intended operation. Confirm these with the owner, relevant specialists and the responsible authorities before finalising acceptance criteria.

The Norwegian Maritime Authority’s [inspection checklist for protected or listed passenger ships](https://www.sdir.no/siteassets/skjema/ks-1057b-psv-passasjersertifikat-for-vernet-eller-fredet-skip--obligatorisk.pdf) is a useful reference for machinery-space housekeeping and documented, time-based and calendar-based maintenance across the machinery installation. Its applicability to Skrei must be established separately.
