## Napier Power Heritage Trust

The [Napier Power Heritage Trust](https://npht.org/) preserves the history of D. Napier & Son and its products. Its English-language website is a useful starting point for Deltic research.

- [Deltic development](https://npht.org/about-napier/products/marine-engines/deltic/): the marine origins and development of the engine family.
- [Series 1 engines](https://npht.org/about-napier/products/marine-engines/deltic/series-1-deltic-engines/): early production variants and their applications.
- [Series 2 engines](https://npht.org/about-napier/products/marine-engines/deltic/series-2-deltic-engines/): development for customers beyond the Admiralty.
- [Series 3 engines](https://npht.org/about-napier/products/marine-engines/deltic/series-3-deltic-engines/): later development of the engine family.

## Additional reading

- [Other Deltic restoration projects](../other-restoration-projects/): documented engine rebuilds and related marine preservation projects, with links and status notes.

- [Institution of Mechanical Engineers: the Deltic and fast patrol boats](https://www.imeche.org/news/news-article/how-the-napier-deltic-engine-fixed-a-serious-problem-for-the-navy%27s-fast-patrol-boats): an engineering institution’s introduction to the marine requirement.
- [Napier Deltic — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napier_Deltic): a general overview, with references for further research. Wikipedia is a community-edited reference, rather than an official project source.

These links are background material. Engine-specific manuals, configuration records and restoration findings will be added as they are identified.
