## From Nasty to the Tjeld class

The Norwegian Tjeld class is closely associated with the Nasty design. According to [KNM Skreis Venneforening’s history](https://knmskrei.no/?page_id=73) (Norwegian), Jan Herman Linge’s concept combined a fast motor torpedo boat with two compact Napier Deltic diesel engines. The prototype Nasty was launched in Mandal in 1957, followed by a Norwegian naval order in 1958.

For English-language context, see [Tjeld-class patrol boat on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tjeld-class_patrol_boat), including its overview of Norwegian service and the related Nasty boats used overseas. This is a general reference, not an official naval history.

## The Napier Deltic

The Deltic is a two-stroke, opposed-piston diesel engine. Three banks of cylinders form a triangle, with a crankshaft at each corner. Its compact layout and low weight made it attractive for fast naval craft. The 18-cylinder version has two pistons per cylinder.

The [Napier Power Heritage Trust’s Deltic history](https://npht.org/about-napier/products/marine-engines/deltic/) explains the marine requirement and the engine’s development. Skrei’s friends association also describes how the Deltic influenced Linge’s vessel concept in its [historical account](https://knmskrei.no/?page_id=73).

Skrei’s main engines have been identified by the project contributor as Napier Deltic T18-37K. See [Main engines](../main-engines/) for the recorded number 534, the photographed casing plate and modification markings, and [Auxiliary machinery](../auxiliary-machinery/) for generator nameplate readings and provisional engine identification. General engine-family descriptions should not be treated as specifications for the installed machinery.
